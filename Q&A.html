SkillSight: demo script and Q&A

What to click, what to say, and the questions to have answers ready for.

Southwest MN Hacks 2026. Schwan's Prompt 01. Ajay Angdembe, solo. Print this or keep it open on a second screen.

Before you start

  • Open index.html, click the green counter in the top right, and clear every ticked plan. Start at zero.
  • Land on the Overview tab, light mode, browser zoom at 100 percent.
  • Run node verify.js once. It should print ALL CHECKS PASSED.
  • Close every other tab. The app needs no internet, so turn wifi off if the venue network is unstable.
  • Have the repo link and FACTS.md open in a second tab in case someone asks for sources.
  • Know your three numbers cold: 20 skills tracked, 7 under 40 percent, 6 held by three people or fewer.

The demo, about 3 minutes

1. Overview40 sec
Start on the Overview. Point at the four numbers, then at the ranked list.

Schwan's runs more than a dozen US plants across four job groups. In this demo, six skills are held by three people or fewer. The list below is not a dashboard, it is an order of work: what to fix first, ranked by how badly it hurts, how few people hold it, and how big the gap is.

2. Number one50 sec
Click row 1, R&D / Food Science - Frozen Dough. One person holds it.

One person at the Marshall R&D centre knows frozen dough formulation. Here is why that matters, what it needs to look like by 2027, and the first step: pair that expert with two mentees. Notice the page also tells me where this data would come from in a real system, which is the question every HR team asks first.

3. Tick it20 sec
Tick "Development plan started". Point at the green Saved chip, then the counter going to 1.

That is saved, not decoration. It survives a reload, it shows in the corner, and it shows on every list that contains this skill.

4. Skills30 sec
Open the Skills tab. Show the three folders, open Critical gap.

Twenty skills, grouped by how well they are covered. Only the critical band is open, because a wall of twenty rows tells you nothing. The worst is the Salina line setup at 12 percent, and that one has no system of record anywhere. That is the finding, not a gap in my tool.

5. Employee view40 sec
Open Employee view, stay on Sam K.

Same data, seen by the person instead of the manager. Sam is one of two people at Marshall who hold the ammonia certification. His step is to pass it on, and the tick box he uses is the same one the manager sees. The challenge asks how employees close skill gaps, so the tool had to speak to the employee, not only about them.

6. Close20 sec
Toggle dark mode, then stop.

Four files. Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no framework, no build step, nothing loaded from the internet, so this cannot break during judging. Every outside number is sourced, and three claims got cut because I could only trace them to blogs.

If something goes wrong

Laptop dies: the deck in exports/ carries the same story in nine slides. Browser misbehaves: refresh with Ctrl+F5, state is in local storage and comes back. Asked to skip ahead: go straight to step 2, it is the strongest 50 seconds.

Questions about the data

Are these real Schwan's numbers?Expect this first

No, and the app says so on its own front page. Coverage percentages, per-site headcounts and employee records are invented. What is real and sourced: the four job groups, which are Schwan's own career areas, the plant and office locations, the Sioux Falls build-out, the Salina expansion and the CJ CheilJedang ownership.

Every claim and its source is listed in FACTS.md in the repo, and inside the app under "Read more" on the Overview.

Where did the 8,500 employees come from?

It is a stated assumption, not a fact. Schwan's does not publish a headcount, and third-party profiles disagree: Wikipedia and IBISWorld say about 8,500, Revelio Labs reports closer to 4,000. I used the high end and labelled it as an assumption rather than claiming it.

A real company would never have this data. How would you actually get it?The big one

Most of it already exists. Every skill page names the systems a real deployment would read from.

The strongest case is ammonia refrigeration. Systems holding 10,000 pounds or more are a covered process under OSHA 1910.119. The employer must refresher-train operators at least every three years and keep a record naming each employee, the date, and how understanding was verified. That roster exists by law. Food safety is similar: a facility's written food safety plan must be prepared, or its preparation overseen, by a preventive controls qualified individual. Maintenance skills come from work order sign-offs, which record who actually did the work rather than who sat through a course.

And one skill honestly says "None. This skill has no system of record anywhere." That is the Salina line setup, and it is the finding.

Your site list does not match ours.

Fair, and the app says the list is compiled from public reporting rather than from a list the company publishes. Schwan's states only that it runs more than a dozen US facilities. If you can give me the real footprint it is one edit in data.js.

Is Sioux Falls a pizza plant?

No, it is 700,000 square feet of Asian-style food production for bibigo, over 600 plant jobs plus 50 office jobs, opening 2027. I had it wrong as a pizza plant earlier in the build and corrected it against the company's own May 2024 press release.

Where did the industry statistics come from?

Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute for the 2.1 million unfilled jobs by 2030 and the $1 trillion cost, with retirement named by 34 percent of manufacturers. The IIAR, the industrial refrigeration association, for the ageing trades: 40 percent of the 12 million skilled trades workers are over 45 and nearly half of those are over 55.

I cut two statistics that only traced back to vendor blogs, including a claim about the average age of ammonia technicians. If I cannot name the source, it is not in the app.

Questions about the scoring

How does the "do this first" ranking work?Know this cold

Three factors multiplied: impact if the knowledge is lost, scored 1 to 5 and divided by 5. Scarcity, as 3 divided by how many people hold it, so one person scores 3 and three people score 1. And gap size, as how far coverage sits below 100, with a floor so a well-covered skill still registers.

In code it is one line: (impact / 5) * (3 / headcount) * (0.4 + gap). Frozen dough wins because it is the only skill held by a single person and it scores 4 of 5 on impact.

Why those weights? They look arbitrary.

They are a judgment, and I would not defend the exact constants. What I defend is the shape: scarcity should dominate, because one person leaving is a different problem from a team being thin, and impact should scale it. In a real deployment those weights are a setting an HR leader tunes, not something a developer decides.

Why does this skill have no plan?

Because plans are written where there is something to act on: coverage under 40 percent, a skill held by three people or fewer, or a 2027 target. The app states that rule on any skill that has no plan, rather than leaving the page blank. Not every skill deserves work.

Questions about the build

Did you use AI to build this?Answer honestly

Yes, AI tools are permitted at this event and I used them. The rule is that I can explain my own code, so ask me anything in it. The architecture decisions are mine: no framework so it runs offline, one page per challenge question, ranking instead of charts, and a self-check script so I am not testing by clicking.

Why no charts?

I built them and threw them away. I had a risk matrix and bar charts using Chart.js, and they looked impressive while telling the viewer nothing they could act on. A ranked list with the reason attached is more useful than a scatter plot, and dropping the library also means the app loads with no internet.

Why no framework?

Four files, no build step, no dependencies, nothing from a CDN. It opens by double-clicking index.html. During judging that means nothing can fail because a network is down, and every line is mine to explain.

How do you know it works?

verify.js renders every page in a stub DOM and asserts: every clickable row opens a real page, every page has a plan or a stated reason it does not, a started plan appears in every list that contains that skill, Previous and Next are correct at all 42 positions, and the data obeys the rule the app states on screen. It caught a real bug where the risk list saved plans under a different key than the skills list.

Where is the data stored?

Ticked plans go to browser local storage, keyed by skill. That is honest but limited: it is per browser, and per skill rather than per person. In a real build it is a row in an HR system with an owner and a date. I would rather say that than pretend the demo is a product.

Questions about the product

Workday and SAP already do this. Why build it?

Those systems store the data. The gap is that they show you everything at once and leave the prioritising to you. This does one thing they do not: it puts the at-risk skills in an order and attaches a first step to each. It is a lens over data an enterprise system already holds, not a replacement for it.

Who actually uses this, and how often?

A plant manager or HR business partner opens it monthly and works the top of the list. An employee opens it when their manager shares their plan. It is not a daily tool, and a tool that pretends to be daily gets ignored.

What is the business case?

The cost of the problem is measurable in a way the fix is not. One certified ammonia technician retiring at a plant with two of them is a production risk at a site that runs continuously. The tool costs a data connection and someone's attention once a month. I would not claim a dollar figure I cannot source.

Is this not just a spreadsheet?

The data could live in a spreadsheet. The ranking, the one-click path from a gap to its first step, and the shared state between manager and employee do not survive in one. But if the honest answer for a 200-person company is a spreadsheet, that is the honest answer.

What about privacy? You are tracking people.Take seriously

The demo shows no real people, and employee names on the profiles are invented. In a real deployment this holds skills and training records, which HR already holds, so the controls are the ones already applied to HR data: access by role, employees able to see their own record, and no performance or disciplinary content anywhere near it. A skills tool that becomes a ranking of people is a tool nobody will give honest data to.

Could the ranking be biased?

Yes, in two ways worth naming. Coverage data is only as good as who got recorded as trained, and informal skills are systematically under-recorded. And impact scores are set by managers, so they carry whatever that manager values. That is an argument for showing the inputs on screen, which the app does, rather than hiding a score behind a badge.

Questions about scope

What is missing?

Real data, obviously. Beyond that: matching people to plans automatically rather than one written step per skill, a real record with an owner and a due date, and a manager's view of their own team rather than the whole company. I would build the team view next, because that is who would actually open it.

The prompt mentions AI career advisors. Where is the AI?

There is none, deliberately. The prompt lists an AI advisor as one option among seven. A chatbot on top of 20 invented rows would have been a bluff, and this demo does not have one anywhere else in it. The ranking is the intelligence, and it is arithmetic I can show you.

Did you do the bonus RFID track?

No. Solo, with one build window, a finished entry beats two half-finished ones. The natural version is a badge tap at a plant that pulls up that person's skills and what they are training toward, which is the Employee view with a scanner in front of it.

What took the longest?

Not the code. Getting the shape right. Three layouts went in the bin, including one I liked, because a wall of charts and a twenty-row table both told the viewer nothing. The last change before judging was grouping skills into folders so only the critical band is open.
