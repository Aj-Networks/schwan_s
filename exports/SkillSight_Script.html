The problem

Set it up

Schwan's runs more than a dozen US plants across four job groups, and a new plant opens in 2027.

But ask a simple question. If the person who knows the ammonia refrigeration system leaves, who else can do it? Today that answer takes phone calls to three plants.

Do Point at the zero. That is the whole problem in one number.

Note Let the quote hang for a beat before the next line.

The finding

Slow down here

Six skills in this demo are held by three people or fewer. One is held by a single person: frozen dough formulation, at the Marshall R&D centre.

Two of these are ammonia refrigeration techs who are close to retirement. That one is not guesswork. Ammonia systems over ten thousand pounds are an OSHA covered process, so the list of trained operators is kept by law. The data already exists.

Do This is the slide people remember. Slow your pace by half.

Note If a Schwan's person is judging, watch for a nod on the OSHA line.

1:15 to 2:00

The answer

The core of the pitch

Most dashboards show numbers and stop. This one scores every at-risk skill on three things: how much it hurts, how few people hold it, and how big the gap is. Then it sorts them.

So a manager does not get a puzzle. They get an order of work. Fix frozen dough first, and here is the first step: pair the expert with two mentees.

Each step is one of the five methods the challenge names. Training, mentoring, certification, job rotation, project experience.

Do If you are demoing live, switch to the app here and tick one plan box.

Note This is the differentiator. If you are running long, cut slide 5, never this one.

The employee

The human beat

The challenge says employees close skill gaps, so one screen is built for the employee, not only about them.

Sam is a refrigeration tech at Marshall, fourteen years in. He is one of two people who hold the ammonia certification, so his step is to pass it on. He ticks the same box his manager sees.

Do If demoing: tick the box, show the counter go to one, then say the last line.

Note Say his name, not the employee. That is the point of the slide.
